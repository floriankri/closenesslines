# Time-spacing experiments

Local experiment for comparing the current centered, two-sided timeline under
three mappings of age to vertical position. The experiment does not change saved
ages, closeness values, public pages or social export.

## Candidates

- Equal years: reference view; easiest to read as elapsed time.
- Compressed childhood: give the early years less room, easing back toward normal
  spacing around age 10 or 12. A storytelling choice for adult timelines.
- Recent years expanded: use relative age with a monotone power curve so the
  later part of the timeline gets more room. Adapts to the person's total age.

An additional possible direction is activity-weighted spacing: expand periods
with life events, new relationships and changes in closeness; compress relatively
stable stretches. This could suit different ages, but depends on how much detail
someone entered and needs care to avoid treating sparse records as unimportant.
A user-controlled focus window with a small full-life overview is another option.

## Constraints

- Preserve chronological order and exact visible age labels.
- Apply the same mapping to people, death/end markers, events and age guides.
- Keep closeness values unchanged and total plot height consistent for comparison.
- State when spacing is unequal; do not present these mappings as a validated
  measure of subjective time or relationship importance.
- Equal space per decade is mathematically equivalent to equal years, so it is
  not a separate candidate.

Recommendation: compare mild childhood compression with recent-years emphasis
before changing the production layout. Keep an equal-years view available.
